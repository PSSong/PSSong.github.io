Over the past few weeks, shifts in financial policy that are gradually intensifying from a neutral standpoint have been drawing attention. In particular, the introduction of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC) and the movement by global asset manager BlackRock toward Bitcoin institutionalization suggest fundamental changes in the financial system. These changes are forming a new paradigm for the financial structure while increasing the possibility of connection with international financial institutions.

The introduction of CBDCs presents the possibility of significantly improving the efficiency of cross-border financial transactions and making the execution of monetary policy more precise. However, this simultaneously possesses a dual nature that could lead to strengthened central bank control and privacy concerns. This is triggering discussions regarding the distribution of power within the existing financial system and individual financial freedom.

BlackRock's movement toward Bitcoin institutionalization demonstrates that cryptocurrencies are entering a stage of being recognized within traditional financial markets. This reflects the process of cryptocurrencies moving beyond being simple investment objects to establishing themselves as core components of the global financial system. In particular, the increasing interest of institutional investors in cryptocurrency is expected to play a role in enhancing the stability and liquidity of the cryptocurrency market.

However, behind these changes lie financial market instability and distrust in the existing monetary system. While BlackRock's movement can be interpreted as part of an effort to strengthen financial market stability, it can also be seen as an attempt to break the existing framework of the financial system. This shift in direction signifies a transition from a neutral stance in financial policy toward a trend that demands more active intervention and change.

These changes go beyond mere technological progress, requiring a new understanding of the international financial order and economic relations between nations. The possibility of connection with international financial institutions poses important questions about how these changes will be coordinated and integrated on a global scale. Continuous observation is required to see how these shifts in financial policy will affect future international economic cooperation and competitive dynamics.
