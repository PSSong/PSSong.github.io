China's recent actions are notable for their multifaceted efforts to expand influence within the East Asian region. Economically, it is strengthening connectivity with regional countries through the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), a large-scale infrastructure project, and politically, it is showing movements to strengthen leadership within the region through security cooperation and diplomatic approaches. These strategies reflect an intention to further solidify its geopolitical status within the Indo-Pacific region, going beyond merely pursuing economic interests.

In particular, China's strategy becomes even more prominent within the competitive framework with the United States. While the Indo-Pacific strategy is progressing toward strengthening networks of security alliances and economic cooperation centered around the United States, China is continuing its efforts to balance this by pursuing close cooperation with countries within the region. This aims to secure sustainable influence in the global geopolitical landscape, moving beyond simple expansion of influence within the region.

However, these movements are eliciting various reactions among neighboring countries. While some nations are strengthening cooperation with China in pursuit of economic benefits, others are taking a cautious approach, expressing concerns over security and sovereignty issues. This suggests that China's expansion strategy is acting as a catalyst for readjusting complex alliance relationships and interests within the region.

Ultimately, China's strategy serves as a crucial element in shaping the future of East Asian geopolitics and can be seen as part of the process of redefining China's role and position on the global stage. This trend is expected to trigger ongoing discussions on how the international community will understand and respond to China's expansion strategy.
