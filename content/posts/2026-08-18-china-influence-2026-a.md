Looking at recent trends, China's digital economy strategy reveals an intention to further expand its influence within the Indo-Pacific region, centered around South Korea. This can be interpreted as a complex movement reflecting geopolitical interests beyond simple economic cooperation.

China is strengthening its relationship with South Korea through cooperation in advanced technology sectors such as 5G communications, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence. Beyond mere technical exchange, this cooperation serves as a crucial link in expanding China's digital economy network into the Indo-Pacific region. In particular, South Korea's technological infrastructure and market accessibility are acting as key elements for China to expand its influence in this region.

In this regard, from South Korea's perspective, it must be recognized that such cooperation brings geopolitical complexities alongside economic benefits. Meanwhile, considering the alliance with the United States, China's strategy further highlights the dilemma facing South Korea. Within the context of its Indo-Pacific strategy, South Korea continues to strive for a balance between its alliance with the U.S. and economic cooperation with China.

Experts advise that "South Korea must closely evaluate the economic benefits it can gain from participating in China's digital economy strategy, alongside the potential geopolitical impacts." Such an approach will help South Korea further solidify its position amidst global technological competition and geopolitical shifts.

Ultimately, China's digital economy strategy demonstrates an attempt to expand its influence within the Indo-Pacific region through South Korea, presenting new challenges and opportunities for East Asian nations, including South Korea.
