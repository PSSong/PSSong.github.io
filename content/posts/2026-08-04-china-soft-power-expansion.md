Over the past two months, China has been further intensifying its strategy to expand soft power through cultural content. This is part of an effort to more effectively disseminate its culture to the global market by emulating successful cases of global cultural content, such as K-pop. These movements are contributing significantly to increasing China's global cultural influence.

Both the Chinese government and private enterprises are expanding investments across various fields, including film, drama, and music. In particular, large-scale production companies are entering the global market by developing content with international-standard storytelling and production quality. For example, Chinese dramas and films are already gaining popularity beyond Asia to Europe and North America, playing a crucial role in raising the international recognition of Chinese culture.

Furthermore, China is actively participating in global cultural festivals and international film festivals to expand cultural exchange. These activities serve as platforms to introduce Chinese values and culture to the world, going beyond mere content export. In particular, the formation of a global fandom centered around the younger generation is acting as a factor that further strengthens China's soft power.

China's strategy does not stop at merely expanding cultural influence; it also contributes to economic benefits and the strengthening of its geopolitical position. Success in the global cultural market helps improve China's international image and assists in solidifying diplomatic relations through cultural exchange with various nations.

In conclusion, China's cultural content export strategy is establishing itself as a core element of soft power expansion, further expanding China's role in the international community through continuous growth and strengthening influence in the global cultural market.
