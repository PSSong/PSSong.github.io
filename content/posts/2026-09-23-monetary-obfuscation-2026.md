The Fed's recent monetary policy announcements are intricately intertwined with mentions of inflation, causing confusion among financial market participants. Until the 29th, the Fed maintained a neutral stance, but has recently shifted toward a more "hawkish" position by emphasizing concerns over inflation. However, in this transition, the Fed is also addressing various other economic issues alongside inflation, making it difficult to form a clear consensus on interest rate hikes.

This may be an effort to reflect the overall complexity of the economy rather than simply addressing inflation alone. Nevertheless, such ambiguity provides uncertainty for investors and economists, making predictions regarding the timing and magnitude of future interest rate hikes even more difficult. It can be interpreted as reflecting a complex situation where the Fed demonstrates its commitment to controlling inflation while simultaneously considering other factors such as economic growth and employment conditions.

Ultimately, this approach by the Fed means that market participants must comprehensively consider various factors. While this may cause short-term uncertainty, it can be interpreted as an effort toward implementing a more balanced economic policy in the long term.
