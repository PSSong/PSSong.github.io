Following the US midterm elections, the accelerating rise in oil prices shows a deep connection to geopolitical movements beyond simple economic factors. This change, which began just three days ago, reflects a shift from an initial positive outlook ('bullish') to an escalation of geopolitical tensions ('escalation').

The possibility of potential attacks on Iran threatens the stability of the Middle East region, leading to increased uncertainty regarding crude oil supply. This situation directly impacts the global economy and presents an even more serious problem for countries with high energy dependency. Rising oil prices are likely to act as a factor that goes beyond simple increases in energy costs, potentially causing inflationary pressure and a slowdown in economic growth.

Behind this shift lies an entanglement of US domestic political situations and the complex geopolitical relationships in the Middle East. Changes in policy direction following the election and the possibility of resetting relations with Iran are providing significant uncertainty to the international community. Therefore, future oil price trends will likely require continuous observation regarding US foreign policy and the stability of the Middle East region.
