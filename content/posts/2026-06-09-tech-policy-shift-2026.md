On June 9, 2026, as Goldman Sachs adjusted its outlook on the obesity drug market from 'neutral' to 'bullish,' the complex relationship between changes in technology policy and economic interests is once again drawing attention. In particular, the popularization of oral GLP-1s drugs stands at the center of this shift.

This transition does not merely reflect an increase in market demand but also implies several factors related to changes in technology policy. Along with research support from agencies such as DARPA, innovations in pharmaceutical technology are positively impacting the obesity drug market. These technological advancements, alongside improvements in medical efficiency, have significantly expanded the potential for economic profit generation.

Goldman Sachs' adjustment in outlook suggests that the market's growth potential has significantly increased as technological progress and policy support combine. However, at the same time, these changes may result in a realignment of the competitive landscape and economic interests within the pharmaceutical industry. Competition between established pharmaceutical companies and new entrants is likely to intensify, and this is expected to become even more complex depending on changes in technological innovation and the regulatory environment.

Such trends serve as a prime example of how technology policy interacts with economic performance. Market participants are expected to closely monitor the interaction between these policy changes and technological advancements and readjust their future investment strategies accordingly.
