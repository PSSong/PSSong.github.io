By recently demanding information sharing from crypto companies in Kenya, the IMF is suggesting that a new paradigm in international financial regulation is unfolding. This move goes beyond the financial policy of a single nation and raises the possibility of a close link between global financial structures and monetary policies.

**Reading Between the Lines**: Observations suggest that this demand goes beyond simple data collection and reflects an international will to more effectively monitor and regulate the global flow of crypto assets. In particular, the IMF's actions can be interpreted as reflecting international concerns regarding the increasing threats to financial stability alongside the growth of the crypto industry.

This approach, starting with Kenya, suggests the possibility of expanded application to other countries in the future. This is expected to trigger global discussions on how the crypto industry can harmonize and coexist with existing financial systems. Especially at a time when a consensus is forming regarding the need for transparency and regulation of crypto assets, this move by the IMF is projected to accelerate the evolution of international financial regulation.

Furthermore, this movement could be part of an attempt to reshape the global financial order amidst the financial competition between the US and China. The global expansion of the crypto industry can be interpreted as a challenge to existing monetary hegemony, and how the IMF responds to these changes will serve as an important indicator of its role.

In conclusion, the IMF's demand for information sharing is not merely about crypto companies in Kenya but can be interpreted as a significant step toward the future of global financial regulation. These changes are expected to contribute to redefining the relationship between the crypto industry and traditional financial systems and establishing a new framework for international financial policy.
