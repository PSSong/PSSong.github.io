A distinct pattern of sector rotation has recently been observed in financial markets. Specifically, the inflow of funds into defensive sectors, including energy and consumer staples, is accelerating. This is interpreted as reflecting investors' tendency to avoid market uncertainty and risk.

This trend, which has persisted since the 19th, initially appeared to reflect a 'bearish' sentiment in the market. However, recently, this flow has been transitioning into a 'de-escalation' phase. This may serve as a signal that market tensions are easing, reflecting an awareness that while investors remain cautious of uncertainty, the risks are gradually diminishing.

These changes are occurring under the complex influence of economic uncertainties and geopolitical factors. The energy sector is regarded as a hedge against risks related to supply instability, while consumer staples are valued as stable investment options due to their characteristic of maintaining demand even during economic downturns.

Investors can discern shifts in market sentiment through these trends. As long as the inflow into defensive sectors continues, the market remains wary of uncertainty; however, the fact that the trend is shifting toward 'de-escalation' can be interpreted as a positive signal. It is necessary to closely monitor how these trends will evolve in response to future economic indicators and policy changes.
