Over the past 13 days, China has expressed a firm stance against U.S. sanctions on Iran and direct sanctions on Chinese companies. This response can be interpreted as more than just a reaction; it may signal a search for new alliance structures amidst geopolitical tensions with the United States. In particular, China's movement to dilute U.S. influence by strengthening economic cooperation with neighboring countries is noteworthy.

However, it seems unlikely that such a strong response will immediately lead to military or political confrontation. Instead, China's actions can be interpreted as an attempt to readjust its relationship with the United States alongside diplomatic pressure. In particular, the possibility of economic alliances appears to be part of China's strategy to strengthen its autonomy on the international stage. For example, the China-led 'Belt and Road Initiative' can be understood as a way to counter U.S. economic influence through an economic network connecting Asia and Europe.

In this regard, China's recent moves are highly likely to be a strategic choice for securing a sustainable position within the global economic order, going beyond mere retaliation against the United States. Nevertheless, for such a strategy to be successfully implemented, harmony with the diverse interests of the international community is essential. Therefore, international attention will likely continue regarding which direction China's response will take in the future.

While tensions are currently escalating, efforts from both sides to avoid extreme confrontation can be detected simultaneously. Therefore, China's response could be a gesture toward immediate diplomatic de-escalation, or it cannot be ruled out that it may be a strategic move to form new geopolitical alliance structures in the long term.
