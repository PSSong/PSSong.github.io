Since El Salvador adopted Bitcoin as legal tender, the role and direction of support from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) have recently come under scrutiny. In particular, the IMF's movement to support donations through private capital for Bitcoin-related projects in El Salvador can be interpreted as a significant signal reflecting a shift from a previously 'bullish' stance to a 'neutral' one.

Until recently, positive views emphasizing the potential for economic innovation and growth through Bitcoin adoption were predominant. However, recent changes suggest that the IMF is taking a more cautious approach. This implies that the initial enthusiasm for Bitcoin adoption is cooling somewhat, and a reassessment of its effects and potential risks is underway.

This strategic shift by the IMF suggests a transition in capital flow from public resources to private capital. This may be part of an effort at the national level to minimize the risks of public funds while simultaneously inducing investment from the private sector. The influx of private donations is moving toward strengthening the sustainability and stability of Bitcoin projects, while also expanding the role of external capital in the national economy.

Such a transition goes beyond mere technical change, reflecting a shift in perception regarding the role of the state and the importance of private capital within the global financial system. El Salvador's case is likely to serve as a reference for other nations considering the adoption of digital currencies in the future. In particular, the process of finding a balance between the limitations of public funds and the flexibility of private capital provides an important lesson.

In this context, the IMF's recent moves demonstrate that El Salvador's Bitcoin strategy is evolving from an initially innovative attempt toward a more balanced approach. Future trends are likely to form a new paradigm regarding the division of roles between public and private capital in the global expansion of Bitcoin and digital currencies.
