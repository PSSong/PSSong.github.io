Over the past six days, as personal conflicts between global tech giants have come to the surface, a pattern of public attention being diverted from AI technology and cybersecurity threats has been observed. These conflicts must be understood within a larger context of the struggle for technological hegemony, beyond mere competition between corporations.

Personal disputes among tech companies often capture public attention, serving to dilute discussions regarding their core technological issues. For instance, recent controversies have centered on personal friction between executives of specific companies, which has, in some respects, pushed critical discussions on AI ethics and security threats to the background. This method effectively disperses the public's cognitive resources, ultimately making it difficult to achieve a deep understanding of and a response to technological threats.

This phenomenon becomes even more complex at the intersection of technological and political issues. Personal conflicts arising during the competition between tech companies can sometimes be interpreted as part of the technological race between nations. Therefore, a close analysis is required to determine how such conflicts will impact global technology policy and the future of AI.

In conclusion, personalized conflicts between tech companies go beyond mere internal corporate matters, playing a role in diluting public perception and complicating responses to technological threats. This can be interpreted as adding a new dimension—cognitive dilution—to the competition surrounding technological hegemony.
