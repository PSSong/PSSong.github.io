The interest rate decision in the recent FOMC meeting clearly revealed the central bank's 'hawkish' stance toward economic conditions. This represents a significant shift from the 'neutral' stance maintained until 30 days ago, reflecting a strong commitment to economic stability and inflation control. This transition suggests that instability in economic indicators and inflationary pressures are persisting.

Behind this change lie persistently high inflation levels and uncertainty regarding economic growth. To prevent economic overheating, the Fed is tightening monetary policy through interest rate hikes, which is expected to impact the lending policies of regional banks and the financial markets as a whole. In particular, rising costs for corporate and consumer loans are likely to impose constraints on economic activity.

However, there is also a view that this 'hawkish' approach could contribute to securing economic stability in the long term. Depending on how future economic indicators and the Fed's policy decisions interact, market reactions and economic outlooks are expected to vary significantly.
