Recently, a noticeable widening of high-yield (HY) credit spreads has been observed in financial markets. This suggests that investors are shifting into risk-aversion mode, reflecting heightened vigilance regarding economic uncertainty. However, this risk-aversion momentum is exhibiting an even more complex pattern amidst discrepancies with other economic indicators.

This shift in stance, which began 30 days ago, started from a neutral position but has recently transitioned toward a more dovish direction. This reflects the tension between expectations for economic recovery and uncertainty, alongside central banks' accommodative policy stances. While the widening of credit spreads indicates concerns about corporate financial health, at the same time, low VIX levels and US 10-year yields show that market participants still expect a relatively stable environment.

These mixed signals demand complex judgment from investors. On one hand, vigilance regarding potential economic risks is intensifying; on the other hand, there are signals that ongoing central bank support and a low-interest-rate environment are maintaining market stability. Consequently, there is an emerging need for investors to readjust their portfolio sector rotations and risk management strategies within this complex economic landscape.

Ultimately, the discrepancy between widening credit spreads and stable VIX and interest rate levels demonstrates a situation that requires close analysis to accurately grasp the current state of the market.
