Over the past 21 days, the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) CBDC experimental project, Agora, has been in the spotlight. Initially, due to technical hurdles and difficulties in cooperation, the issue entered an 'escalation' phase; however, recent progress has reversed this direction to 'bullish'. This change goes beyond mere technical success, serving as an opportunity to reaffirm the importance of cooperation among central banks within the global financial system.

Project Agora is a platform where central banks jointly conduct experiments and development for digital currencies, and its success has significantly increased the possibility of CBDC adoption. The achievements gained by overcoming previous hurdles are deeply meaningful in several aspects. First, it proved the practicality and stability of CBDCs by resolving technical difficulties. Second, it demonstrated that the cooperation model between central banks is effective, providing a positive outlook for similar international projects in the future.

Particularly noteworthy is the background of this transition. Along with initial technical difficulties, differences in regulatory environments and policies among various countries acted as major obstacles; however, the success of cooperation emerging from overcoming these issues suggests that central banks can collaborate more closely toward common goals. This is expected to serve as a driver for accelerating innovation in the global payment system.

This change can be interpreted as an important step in reshaping the future of the financial system, beyond simple technological progress. Central banks have now gained more confidence in building a more efficient and secure payment system through CBDC adoption. The future outlook is optimistic. The success of Project Agora increases expectations that CBDC adoption will go beyond mere technical challenges to open a new paradigm for global financial cooperation.
