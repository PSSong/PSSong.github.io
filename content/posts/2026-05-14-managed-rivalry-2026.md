While the technological competition between the US and China has maintained a structure of managed confrontation over the past few weeks, the situation has recently begun to shift subtly. Until six days ago, tensions between the two nations were on a downward trend, but they are now showing signs of escalating once again. This shift in direction can be interpreted as more than just the superficial impact of technological gaps and trade wars; it can be seen as part of a complex strategy by both nations to manage political tensions.

This structure of managed confrontation is particularly prominent in the fields of AI and semiconductors. While continuing to compete through technological innovation, both nations are simultaneously making efforts to protect corporate interests and alleviate political tensions. However, the recent resurgence of tension suggests that the strategic interests between the US and China are becoming increasingly complex.

In particular, as recent moves have emerged—such as the US strengthening sanctions against Chinese companies or China restricting access to US technology—these changes demonstrate that a lack of trust and mutual suspicion still exists between the two nations. Nevertheless, the fact that this confrontation is not spiraling completely out of control suggests that both sides recognize the necessity of managing tensions.

Such a situation reflects that the relationship between the US and China is not simply a dichotomy between 'hostility' and 'cooperation,' but rather a complex dynamic subtly adjusted between the two. Future trends are expected to continue changing depending on the political judgments and economic interests of both nations.
