Recently, new questions have been raised in financial markets regarding the independence of the Federal Reserve. In particular, a hypothesis is gaining attention that international financial policy coordination through the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) may constrain the Fed's independence. This implies that the Fed's ability to independently assess and respond to domestic economic conditions could be limited.

This issue, which had maintained a neutral stance until recently, has recently shifted from 'neutral' to 'hawkish.' This reflects the market's interpretation that the Fed may adopt a more aggressive monetary policy. Such a change can be interpreted as a signal that measures to curb inflation may be intensified. However, there is still a lack of clear evidence regarding how much the connection with the BIS actually constrains the Fed's independence.

The background of this shift is largely driven by increasing global economic uncertainty and inflationary pressures. The strengthening of international financial regulation and cooperation is complicating the impact on the domestic economy, which ultimately makes predictions regarding inflation and dollar return even more difficult. Therefore, it is necessary to closely monitor future movements and verify this hypothesis through additional evidence and data.
