In a recent announcement, the IMF emphasized the risks within the global bond market, raising serious concerns about financial stability. This warning demonstrates a shift from a previously neutral stance toward a more firm position, leading economic experts to interpret this change as suggesting a potential shift in the direction of financial policy rather than being a mere warning.

Until just ten days ago, the IMF maintained a relatively stable view on the economic situation. However, in its recent announcement, the organization's stance has shifted, highlighting inflation pressures alongside the rapid increase in national debt and the negative impact that large-scale borrowing for Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology may have on sovereign bond markets. This change reflects a serious recognition of the structural problems facing the global economy.

In particular, inflation accelerates rising prices, which further intensifies the pressure on central banks to raise interest rates. This is highly likely to lead to a decline in bond values. Furthermore, the increase in national debt threatens long-term fiscal health, and since AI-related borrowing could exacerbate future fiscal burdens, the IMF's warning carries even greater weight.

The background of this warning lies in the increasing uncertainty within the global economy. The shift in the IMF's position can be interpreted as a signal for governments and central banks to re-examine economic policies and prepare more proactive countermeasures. Consequently, these warnings from the IMF are expected to play a significant role in future financial policy-making processes.
