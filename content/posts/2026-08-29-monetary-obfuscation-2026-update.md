The Fed's interest rate policy has recently shown a subtle shift in direction, sending complex signals to global financial markets. The Fed, which maintained a neutral stance until just 30 days ago, has recently shifted toward a more hawkish attitude, making the impact on domestic inflation possibilities and international financial structures even more confusing.

**Changes in Interest Rate Policy and Its Background**
High inflationary pressure still underlies the Fed's strengthening of its interest rate hike stance. However, the phenomenon of the dollar index falling despite recent rate hikes is unusual. This may reflect the market's interpretation that interest rate hikes alone cannot completely suppress inflation. Furthermore, the narrowing of HY (High Yield) credit spreads suggests that the market feels a certain level of stability regarding the Fed's policy. However, this also serves as a factor that prevents complete relaxation of vigilance against inflation risks.

**Dollar Flow and International Financial Structure**
The decline in the dollar index is sending interesting signals to international financial markets. While this does not mean that confidence in the U.S. economy is being completely shaken, it suggests that the growth of other major economies or changes in their monetary policies are affecting the relative value of the dollar. In particular, the impact of economic trends in China and the European Union on the value of the dollar is becoming increasingly significant.

**Inflation Possibilities and Future Outlook**
The complex interaction between the Fed's interest rate policy and dollar flow is making it even more difficult to predict domestic inflation possibilities. In a situation where inflationary pressures persist, if the Fed adopts a stronger interest rate hike policy, it may help curb inflation in the short term but could burden economic growth in the long term. On the other hand, the possibility that a decline in the value of the dollar could accelerate inflation through rising import prices cannot be ruled out.

Such a complex situation means that financial market participants must analyze the Fed's policy direction more closely and prepare for various scenarios. The Fed's policy changes do not only affect the domestic economy but also play a pivotal role in maintaining the delicate balance between the global financial structure and inflationary pressures.
