Over the past three days, as tensions between the United States and Iran have shifted toward easing, financial markets have maintained stability without significant fluctuations in crude oil prices, contrary to expectations. This suggests that beyond the simple impact of geopolitical events on oil prices, there is a complex entanglement of capital liquidity and market psychological factors.

**Background and Meaning of Easing Tensions**  
Previously, the confrontation between the two sides amplified concerns regarding energy supply stability in the Middle East, increasing the likelihood of rising oil prices. However, recent easing appears to reflect changes in strategic judgment by both parties alongside international mediation efforts. In particular, a perception has spread throughout the market that the possibility of Iran's oil supply being completely cut off has decreased due to the ongoing impact of economic pressure and sanctions, as well as the diversification of the global energy market.

**Capital Liquidity and Market Sentiment**  
The stability of crude oil prices reflects more than just the balance of supply and demand; it mirrors the liquidity of financial markets and the psychological state of investors. In the current situation, as geopolitical tensions ease, investors appear to be focusing more on long-term economic prospects rather than short-term risk aversion. This emphasizes that crude oil prices do not merely react to the immediate impact of events but reflect overall market confidence alongside complex economic factors.

**Changes in the Global Energy Market**  
Furthermore, structural changes in the global energy market are noteworthy. The increase in U.S. shale gas production and the growth of the renewable energy sector are acting as factors that reduce dependency on crude oil. These changes are contributing to maintaining the stability of oil prices despite geopolitical instability in the Middle East.

In conclusion, the phenomenon of stable crude oil prices amidst easing US-Iran tensions is a complex signal reflecting capital liquidity, market sentiment, and structural changes in the energy market, going beyond being a simple direct result of the event itself. In this context, future trends in crude oil prices must pay closer attention to the interaction between economic factors as well as geopolitical ones.
