October 18, 2026 - Amidst the ongoing US fiscal deficit, the recent ambiguity in fiscal policy has become even more prominent. This can be interpreted as an attempt to adjust public perception by depicting the fiscal deficit through a consistent frame across various media outlets. In particular, this issue is adding a new dimension of complexity as the direction shifts from a previous "de-escalation" stance toward a "neutral" one.

While the continuous increase in the fiscal deficit raises economic concerns, it is noteworthy that the way it is depicted is steering public perception in a specific direction. This "manufactured consensus" plays a role in limiting citizens' critical perspectives to some extent by having the government and media jointly emphasize the necessity and limitations of fiscal policy. Ultimately, this appears to be part of an effort to secure public consent for fiscal policy.

The significance of this shift lies in the fact that moving from a de-escalation stance to a neutral stance is inducing market and public attention to focus more on solutions or economic impacts rather than the deficit itself. This suggests a strategic approach to steering public discourse in a specific direction while maintaining ambiguity in fiscal policy.

Ultimately, this policy ambiguity plays a crucial role in regulating public understanding and response to economic issues. Investors and citizens must recognize the need to deeply understand and judge the true meaning of fiscal policy within this context.
