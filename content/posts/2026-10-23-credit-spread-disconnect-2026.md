In recent financial markets, a noticeable divergence is emerging between the widening of HY (high-yield) credit spreads and the stability of the VIX (Volatility Index). This can be interpreted not merely as a crossover of economic indicators, but as a signal reflecting the complex psychological and structural instabilities faced by investors. 

This phenomenon began to appear prominently starting 30 days ago, and previously, this divergence did not receive much attention within a neutral market atmosphere. However, as the market has recently shifted from a 'neutral' stance to a 'hawkish' one, this divergence has taken on even greater significance. This shift suggests that central bank tightening policies are intensifying, which in turn shows a weakening of confidence in high-risk assets.

While investors are expressing concerns regarding corporate financial health through the widening credit spreads, the stability of the VIX index signals that immediate fear or anxiety across the entire market remains limited. This divergence suggests that the market is being complexly influenced by various factors. In particular, the combination of central bank policy changes and uncertainty surrounding economic growth appears to be strengthening investors' risk-aversion tendencies.

Ultimately, the divergence between HY credit spreads and the VIX index can be interpreted as more than a simple discrepancy in data; it serves as an important indicator reflecting the uncertainty and instability regarding the future faced by investors. This phenomenon will serve as crucial reference material for predicting future market trends and adjusting investment strategies.
