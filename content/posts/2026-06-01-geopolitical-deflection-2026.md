As tensions between the US and Iran escalate and energy prices rise, amplifying geopolitical risks in the Middle East, analyses suggest that these events are being used as a strategy to divert market attention from liquidity and credit market trends. The recent shift from an initial neutral stance to escalation is creating an even more complex market environment.

As conflicts in the Middle East have intensified over the past 13 days, energy prices have shown an upward trend. This situation is effectively focusing the attention of market participants on inflation pressures and energy price volatility. However, this essentially functions to highlight concerns regarding an economic slowdown, tending to emphasize uncertainty in economic growth rather than actual inflationary pressure.

The utilization of such geopolitical events produces a strategic effect that shifts the market's focus away from the health of liquidity and credit markets. While trends in liquidity and credit markets are crucial indicators reflecting the fundamental health of the economy, the Middle East conflict and rising energy prices are playing a role in diverting attention from these indicators.

In particular, the recent trend of escalation reflects a change from the initial neutral state, demonstrating that the increase in geopolitical risk is further expanding the psychological impact on the market. This change results in investors paying greater attention to concerns over an economic slowdown, which acts as a factor increasing uncertainty regarding the long-term economic outlook. Therefore, investors need to closely analyze the complex impact that geopolitical risks and energy price volatility have on market trends.
