As the trade dispute between the US and China has recently reignited, uncertainty regarding the global economy is escalating. With tensions between the two nations intensifying over the past few weeks, this is going beyond mere economic friction between the two countries and is having a significant impact on global supply chains. This reignition of tension is forcing companies to accelerate supply chain diversification and re-examine their risk management strategies.

In particular, tariff increases and technological sanctions between the two nations are directly impacting global manufacturing and the IT industry. As the vulnerabilities of supply chains become more prominent, companies are showing movements to break away from structures that are excessively dependent on China. This is promoting the relocation of production bases to Southeast Asia or the Indo-Pacific region, and in this process, new economic links are being formed.

However, these changes will not occur in the short term, and the increased costs and operational disruptions that may occur during the transition process must be endured. Furthermore, concerns are being raised that if tensions between the US and China persist, the growth engine of the global economy could be weakened. 

This situation also presents important challenges to international economic policymakers. It is a time when a new framework must be sought to maintain supply chain stability and economic cooperation. The restructuring of global supply chains is expected to affect not only economic efficiency but also geopolitical balance and international relations.

Ultimately, the reignition of US-China trade tensions is serving as an opportunity to once again emphasize the importance of supply chain resilience and diversification to both international corporations and governments. While these changes may cause short-term instability, they have the potential to be a significant turning point toward a more stable and diversified global economic structure in the long term.
