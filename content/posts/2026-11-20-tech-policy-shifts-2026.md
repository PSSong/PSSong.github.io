As the United States strengthens regulations in the semiconductor and artificial intelligence (AI) sectors, the technological competition between the US and China is expanding beyond simple corporate rivalry to the level of national infrastructure. This field, which had a prevailing positive outlook until just 15 days ago, has recently shifted sharply toward "escalation." This shift reflects the rising tensions surrounding technological superiority between the two nations. In particular, the strengthening of regulations linked to the BIS semiconductor ban is further restricting Chinese companies' access to global supply chains, expanding the frontlines of technological competition.

This change is not limited solely to the technology sector but illustrates a larger picture related to the redistribution of geopolitical influence. These US measures can be interpreted as part of an effort to strengthen dominance over global technological standards and infrastructure, alongside the intention to suppress China's technological advancement. Therefore, future trends in technology regulation are expected to further complicate the strategic competition structure between the US and China.

Market participants should closely monitor these changes and pay attention to the global strategies of related companies and the restructuring of supply chains. The strengthening of technology regulations foreshadows structural changes in the future global technological ecosystem.
