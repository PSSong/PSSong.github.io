In recent months, China's geopolitical influence in the Pacific region has been noticeably expanding. In particular, as economic aid and large-scale infrastructure investments in Pacific island nations surge, China's role in this region is being further strengthened. These movements prompt a re-examination of the importance of East Asian and Indo-Pacific strategies within the context of the US-China competition.

China is strengthening economic ties through various forms of aid and investment in Pacific island nations. This reflects a strategic intention to secure political support within the region and expand control over maritime routes, going beyond the mere pursuit of economic interests. For example, infrastructure projects range from the establishment of communication networks to investments in energy facilities, all of which are linked to China's maritime strategy.

These trends provide implications for South Korea and its Indo-Pacific strategy. Considering its geopolitical position in East Asia and the Indo-Pacific region, there is a growing need for South Korea to reconsider its response strategies to these Chinese movements. In particular, multilateral cooperation projects led by or involving South Korea can play a crucial role in responding to China's expanding influence while simultaneously strengthening regional stability and economic cooperation.

Experts warn that if China's activities within the Pacific region continue, the geopolitical landscape of this area could fundamentally change. This implies the possibility of intensified competition between the US and China, emphasizing the importance of strategic alliances and cooperation for Indo-Pacific nations, including South Korea. Therefore, South Korea must closely monitor the changes in this region and strive to maintain and develop a stable geopolitical position through the strengthening of multilateral and security cooperation.
