On November 22, 2026, the Fed's recent shift in policy direction implies a complex interaction with the international financial structure, rather than merely reflecting domestic economic conditions. In particular, the impact of connectivity with the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) on the Fed's independence is drawing significant attention. The transition of the Fed's policy stance from 'hawkish' to 'neutral' over the past 30 days is being interpreted as one of the factors amplifying market uncertainty.

This shift may reflect pressures and adjustments within the international financial system, rather than simply reflecting the easing of domestic inflationary pressures. The BIS focuses on maintaining financial stability through close cooperation with central banks worldwide. In this context, speculations are being raised that the Fed's policy decisions align with BIS recommendations or international requirements for financial stability.

The widening of credit spreads can be interpreted as an indicator reflecting market participants' concerns regarding future economic uncertainty. There are observations that the Fed's shift in policy direction is failing to mitigate this uncertainty and is instead playing a role in further amplifying it. This suggests that the influence of international organizations such as the BIS may act in a manner that constrains the Fed's independence.

Such circumstances emphasize that the Fed's policy decision-making process is exposed to external pressures. However, this may not simply be determined by external pressure alone, but could also be a process of finding a balance between international financial stability and domestic economic conditions. Therefore, the shift in the Fed's policy direction appears to reflect an adjustment process within a complex global economic environment, rather than being a simple movement in one direction.

Market participants will need to observe these changes closely and consider the impact of connectivity with the BIS and the international financial structure. This is expected to trigger further discussions regarding the Fed's independence and the transparency of its policy decisions.
