May 17, 2026. Amid rising geopolitical instability, investment in energy midstream infrastructure is gaining attention. In particular, the persistent risk in the Strait of Hormuz is amplifying uncertainty regarding the global energy supply chain, leading to an increase in the value of assets related to energy transportation and storage.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical route for global oil transportation, and the international energy market suffers significant shocks whenever tensions rise in this region. The instability in this area over recent weeks has further highlighted the vulnerability of the energy supply chain. This situation does more than just cause fluctuations in oil prices; it emphasizes the need for investment to ensure the stability and efficiency of energy infrastructure.

Interest is particularly growing in the energy midstream sector—the infrastructure responsible for transportation and storage. Midstream assets are key elements in ensuring the stable movement of energy resources, and their importance becomes more pronounced as geopolitical risks increase. This can be interpreted as an issue directly linked to global economic stability, moving beyond mere energy security.

Investors are no longer focusing solely on crude oil price fluctuations but are now considering investments in infrastructure that guarantees the stability of the energy supply chain. This shift is likely to lead to strategic investments aimed at strengthening energy security, not only for energy companies but also at the national level.

In conclusion, geopolitical factors such as the Strait of Hormuz risk are acting as a driver for the increasing value of energy midstream infrastructure assets. This can be interpreted as part of global efforts to build a stable energy supply chain amidst energy geopolitical crises.
