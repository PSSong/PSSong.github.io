The shift in China's AI strategy, which has been continuously discussed over the past three months, symbolizes a new stage in the technological competition between the United States and China. While previous competitive structures focused primarily on securing technological superiority, the current change is moving toward navigating the complex balance between international cooperation and competition.

At the core of this shift lies China's effort to expand its influence on the international stage through the development and application of AI technology, while simultaneously demonstrating a will to strengthen technical standardization and regulatory cooperation through global partnerships. This approach aims for more than just pursuing technological dominance; it seeks to secure a sustainable position within the global technological ecosystem.

In particular, this strategic shift by China carries even greater significance within the geopolitical context of the Indo-Pacific region. As the competition between the U.S. and China emerges as a core element of the Indo-Pacific strategy, China is showing movements to strengthen its influence within the region through AI technology. This has the potential to lead beyond mere technological competition toward a reconfiguration of regional security and economic partnerships.

However, these changes also provoke reactions from the international community. While Western nations maintain a sense of vigilance regarding China's technological expansion, they are simultaneously paying attention to the possibilities for cooperation in the field of AI. This can be interpreted as part of an effort to maintain a delicate balance between technological competition and cooperation.

In conclusion, China's shift in AI strategy presents a new paradigm for global technological competition and will serve as an important indicator for predicting the future direction of international relations and technological cooperation. Through these changes, we can gain a deeper understanding of how technological innovation and geopolitical interests intersect.
