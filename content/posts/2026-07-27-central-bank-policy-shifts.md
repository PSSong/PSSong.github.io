Over the past ten days, financial markets have been in a state of tension regarding the direction of central bank policy. Initially, hawkish measures to curb inflation and support economic growth were expected, but the atmosphere has recently shifted following a proposal from a working group led by Chair Kevin Warsh. This proposal demonstrates the central bank's intention to maintain economic stability through gradual policy adjustments.

Several factors are driving this transition. First, as global economic uncertainty increases, central banks are adopting a more cautious approach. Second, as inflationary pressures show signs of easing more than expected, there is a growing perception that gradual adjustments are necessary rather than abrupt tightening policies. Beyond mere changes in economic indicators, these shifts can be interpreted as part of an effort to find a balance between the stability of the global financial system and economic growth.

Notably, this transition does not merely reflect internal economic factors but is also the result of considering interactions with the international financial environment. Observations suggest that the competitive dynamics between the U.S. and China, along with geopolitical shifts in the Indo-Pacific region, are influencing central bank policy decisions. In this context, gradual adjustment can be interpreted as a strategy to pursue stable growth amidst global economic uncertainty.

Ultimately, these changes by the central bank go beyond a simple modification of policy direction, offering a new interpretation of future economic prospects. Future announcements and policy decisions are expected to further clarify the direction of these gradual adjustments. Market participants recognize through these changes that the central bank is pursuing both economic flexibility and stability simultaneously.
