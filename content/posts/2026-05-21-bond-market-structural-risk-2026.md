Signs of structural erosion in the bond market are becoming more prominent due to recent increases in inflationary pressures and interest rate uncertainty. This issue, which maintained a neutral stance until just two days ago, has now shifted toward a bearish outlook. This change is acting as a factor that accelerates the decline in the value of traditional bond assets.

The persistent rise in inflation is increasing the likelihood that central banks will accelerate interest rate hikes, which puts direct pressure on bond yields. In particular, long-term bonds are more vulnerable to rising interest rates, leading to a strengthening trend of risk aversion among investors. This situation is eroding the value of bond portfolios and prompting investors to consider reallocation toward more stable assets.

However, this negative shift can also be interpreted as a signal requiring investors to take a more cautious approach to the future economic environment. Amidst the uncertainty of inflation and interest rates, investors are expected to increase their interest in diversification strategies and high-yield assets.

In conclusion, the structural risks in the bond market stem from the uncertainty of inflation and interest rates, emphasizing the need for new risk management strategies for investors. While reflecting market instability, these changes serve as an important indicator urging the readjustment of future investment strategies.
