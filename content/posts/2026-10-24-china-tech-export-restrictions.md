In recent weeks, a significant wave of change has been blowing through the global semiconductor supply chain as the Chinese government strengthens technology export restrictions. These measures go beyond simple export controls, playing a role in further entrenching the technological competition between the U.S. and China. The semiconductor industry, in particular, stands at the center of these changes, and China's restrictive measures are emphasizing the need for supply chain reconfiguration for companies worldwide.

This strengthening of restrictions can be interpreted as part of an effort to promote China's technological self-reliance. At the same time, however, global companies are accelerating movements to reduce their dependence on China and seek alternative sources. When viewed in the context of the Indo-Pacific strategy—extending beyond East Asia—this can also be interpreted as part of a strategic move by the United States and its allies to check China's technological dominance.

Market participants must carefully observe how these changes will affect semiconductor price volatility, the pace of technological innovation, and global economic uncertainty in the long term. China's technology export restrictions go beyond a mere economic issue to form a new front in geopolitical competition, which is expected to accelerate the structural reorganization of the global supply chain.
