July 15, 2026—As the application of AI technology in the financial sector expands rapidly, major financial institutions such as Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase are recording notable increases in profits. This shift suggests that we are entering a stage that goes beyond simple technological progress, redefining the understanding of control structures and market trends within the financial markets.

In particular, according to a recent report from Goldman Sachs, the introduction of automated trading and risk management systems through AI-based algorithms has maximized trading efficiency and significantly enhanced the ability to predict minute market fluctuations. This technological advancement allows financial institutions to analyze and predict market trends with greater precision, opening up the possibility of more effectively controlling market flows.

However, these changes are raising concerns regarding market fairness and transparency. The sophistication of AI technology may further deepen information asymmetry, potentially leading to disadvantageous situations for small-scale investors. Specifically, as the term "market-manipulation" emerges, concerns are being raised that AI-driven trading strategies could be used as tools to artificially manipulate the market.

In this context, the success of Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase goes beyond the performance of individual companies, triggering discussions on the direction of technology policy and the necessity of financial regulation. Governments and regulatory bodies are feeling the need to strengthen appropriate supervision and regulation to ensure that such technological advancements do not undermine market fairness.

Ultimately, the entry of AI technology into financial markets brings both new opportunities and potential risks. Since technological progress holds the potential to deepen inequality among market participants while simultaneously increasing market efficiency, future policy approaches will become increasingly important.
