The ambiguity revealed in the Federal Reserve's recent policy-making process is injecting new uncertainty into financial markets. As the Fed, which had maintained a neutral stance until 30 days ago, suddenly shifted toward a hawkish direction, it has become even more difficult for market participants to predict the future direction of policy. This shift is becoming further complicated within the context of its relationship with the Bank for International Settlements (BIS). The Fed's decisions, linked to the BIS's position, provide more room for various interpretations among market participants, which is manifesting as an expansion of credit spreads and a rise in the VIX volatility index.

The widening of credit spreads reflects investors' caution regarding high-risk bonds, while the rise in the VIX index suggests an increase in overall market uncertainty. This phenomenon goes beyond simple changes in economic indicators, demonstrating the psychological impact that ambiguity in the policy-making process has on the market. In a situation where the Fed's policy direction remains unclear, its interaction with the BIS is acting as a factor that further amplifies financial market uncertainty.

In this context, investors are expected to react even more sensitively to future Fed policy announcements and changes in the BIS's stance. If market uncertainty persists, there is a high possibility that demand for stable assets will increase. Therefore, the future movements of the Fed and the BIS are observed to be critical factors that will determine the stability and direction of financial markets.
