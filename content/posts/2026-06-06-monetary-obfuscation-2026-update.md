Global financial markets are exhibiting various discrepancies even within a RISK-OFF environment. This is taking on an even more complex aspect in conjunction with recent shifts in monetary policy direction. This change, which began in early June 2026, initially seemed to strengthen the 'hawkish' stance of central banks, but has recently shown a transition toward a neutral direction. This transition reflects a phenomenon where market participants exhibit a de-risking tendency while simultaneously maintaining a high preference for investment in certain sectors.

The rise in the VIX index reflects market uncertainty and risk-aversion sentiment; however, in contrast, the technology and healthcare sectors are showing strength. In particular, the decline in BTC is further highlighting the risk-aversion tendency toward digital assets. This phenomenon can be interpreted as a representative case showing the discrepancy between traditional risk aversion and investment trends.

The widening of HY credit spreads clearly demonstrates the de-risking tendency in the corporate bond market. This suggests that investors are moving funds into safer assets. However, despite this overall de-risking movement, the strength of certain sectors suggests that various drivers coexist within the market.

These discrepancies reflect the complex interaction between changes in central bank policies and the reactions of market participants. To predict future market trends, it will be necessary to comprehensively analyze these multifaceted data points. In particular, it is necessary to closely observe the impact of the neutral transition in monetary policy on various sectors of the market.
