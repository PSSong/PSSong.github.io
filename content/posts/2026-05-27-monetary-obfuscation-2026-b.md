Over the past few weeks, global financial markets have continued to maintain a risk-on sentiment despite geopolitical uncertainties and cost pressures on corporations. However, contrary to this optimistic market atmosphere, the actual liquidity environment is gradually contracting, suggesting instability in asset prices. In particular, since May 10, 2026, this divergence has become more pronounced as central bank policy stances have shifted from 'dovish' to 'neutral'.

This transition can be interpreted as a signal of central banks' efforts to maintain economic stability while simultaneously preparing for future economic uncertainties. While the previous dovish stance implied accommodative monetary policy to promote economic growth, the recent shift to neutral reflects a more cautious approach to the economic situation. This suggests that while market participants are still focused on optimistic narratives, central banks are recognizing a more complex and uncertain economic environment.

Signs of liquidity contraction can be identified in several areas. Central bank liquidity provision is decreasing, and the fluidity of funds between financial institutions is becoming limited. These changes are highly likely to lead to increased asset price volatility along with rising financing costs in the market. In particular, as the financing environment for corporations worsens, cost pressures may intensify, which could ultimately have a negative impact on corporate earnings.

Market participants should not overlook these liquidity risks. If the current optimistic market atmosphere persists, a liquidity shortage could trigger asset price instability more rapidly than expected. Therefore, investors must carefully monitor changes in central bank policies and the liquidity environment.

In conclusion, the divergence between the market's risk-on sentiment and the actual liquidity environment is emerging as a significant risk factor for the future financial market. The central bank's shift to a neutral stance reflects this uncertainty and calls for a more cautious approach from investors.
