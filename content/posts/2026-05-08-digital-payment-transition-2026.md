Over the past few weeks, as cyber threats and geopolitical instability have escalated globally, the transition to digital payment infrastructure is accelerating further. Until four days ago, this transition was perceived as proceeding under a positive outlook, but recent circumstances have shifted that direction toward 'escalation.' This reflects an urgent need to respond to security threats and a lack of trust between nations, going beyond mere technological advancement.

Companies like Ripple and JPMorgan are at the center of these changes. Ripple's blockchain-based payment system improves the transparency and efficiency of international transactions while providing more robust security against cyberattacks. JPMorgan is also intensifying its efforts to strengthen the stability of the financial system through its own digital payment platform. The movements of these companies reflect the necessity of building secure and reliable financial networks amidst geopolitical instability, transcending simple technological progress.

However, there are points to be cautious about during this transition process. Along with technological progress, a continuous response to security threats is required, and cooperation between governments and corporations is becoming increasingly important. This raises fundamental questions about the stability and sustainability of the global financial system, beyond the mere construction of digital payment infrastructure.

Ultimately, the transition to digital payment infrastructure, accelerated by cyber threats and geopolitical instability, heralds a structural change in the future financial system, moving beyond simple technological shifts.
