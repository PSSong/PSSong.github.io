As the Fed's interest rate policy has recently undergone a change in direction, financial markets have entered an even more confusing state. Having maintained a hawkish stance until 30 days ago, the Fed is now shifting toward a neutral position, leaving market participants struggling to interpret the Fed's true intentions through various indicators amidst this uncertainty.

The rise in HY credit spreads and the increase in the VIX index reflect investor anxiety. A widening credit spread is interpreted as a signal of heightened risk in corporate bonds, while an increase in the VIX index indicates rising market volatility and uncertainty regarding the future. However, in contrast to these elements of anxiety, the strength of the dollar index and the increase in net liquidity add layers of complexity. A strong dollar suggests increased demand for safe-haven assets, which may reflect a lack of confidence in the global economy. Conversely, while the increase in net liquidity implies the continuity of economic activity, it raises questions about how this can be reconciled with the impact of interest rate hikes.

It is not easy to accurately interpret Fed signals amidst such complex indicators. The background behind the Fed's shift from a hawkish stance to a neutral position appears to be driven by changes in the global economic landscape and the easing of inflationary pressures. This transition suggests both the Fed's ability to regulate the market and its policy flexibility for the future. However, the impact of these changes on the market remains difficult to predict. To grasp the Fed's true intentions, greater attention must be paid to forthcoming data and policy announcements.
