A senior official at JP Morgan recently stated that U.S. military superiority supports the dollar's global currency status. These remarks go beyond mere economic facts, suggesting a deep connection between military power and the credibility of a currency. Military superiority enhances a nation's stability and predictability, which serves as a factor in strengthening the stability and reliability of its currency on the international stage.

Notably, this message has been continuously emphasized over the past few weeks. The trend, which has persisted for 19 days, implies an intention to maintain economic influence through military superiority. This can be interpreted as a strategic move not just to maintain currency value, but to continuously reinforce the U.S.'s leading role within the global financial system.

However, this connection is double-edged. While military stability plays a role in supporting currency stability, on the other hand, the possibility of negative impacts on currency markets if military tensions escalate cannot be ruled out. Therefore, it is necessary to pay attention to the complex interaction between military actions and monetary policy.

The timing and repetitive emphasis of these remarks reflect an intention to reaffirm the U.S.'s position in the international financial order through military superiority. This provides important implications for global economic actors to understand the subtle link between military trends and currency values and to make strategic decisions accordingly.
