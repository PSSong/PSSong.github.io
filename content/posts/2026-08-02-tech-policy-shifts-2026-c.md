In the midst of recent major trends in global financial policy, interest in the introduction of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC) is rising once again. In particular, this movement is being interpreted as an implication of potential changes surrounding the global dominance of the US dollar. Until recently, it appeared that efforts to strengthen the dollar's dominance were forming a competitive landscape alongside the movements of various countries toward introducing digital currencies. However, recent trends show that this issue is shifting direction from 'escalation' to 'de-escalation.'

Behind this transition lies the uncertainty of the global economy, along with efforts by various nations to further strengthen their own financial systems. In particular, the introduction of digital currencies is emerging as a strategic card to respond to the fragmentation of existing financial systems. The progress in discussions regarding CBDC introduction by major countries, including the United States, carries significance beyond simply following the trend of digitalization. It reflects an intention to adapt to the dollar-centric international financial system while simultaneously securing competitiveness in a new economic order.

However, clear policy signals have not yet emerged. Nevertheless, these movements are regarded as important tracking elements for future global financial policy. Discussions on CBDC introduction in various countries go beyond mere technological progress, implying the possibility of a realignment of international economic relations, which is leading to diverse interpretations regarding the future of the dollar system.
