Recent news that the U.S. and China are conducting negotiations on artificial intelligence (AI) safety issues has once again highlighted the delicate balance between technological competition and cooperation. While optimistic prospects for potential cooperation between the two sides initially predominated, over the past 12 days, this issue has shifted from a 'Bullish' stance of cooperation toward a phase of tension characterized by 'Escalation.'

At the heart of this shift lies the struggle for technological hegemony between the two nations. As advancements in AI technology will have a decisive impact on future economies and security, both countries possess a strong will to secure dominance in this field. As both sides express concerns regarding each other's technological access and controls during the negotiation process, uncertainty surrounding the possibility of cooperation is increasing.

In particular, concerns over technological security and data protection are emerging as core issues in the negotiations. For both nations to reach an agreement on the safety and ethical use of AI technology, building mutual trust is essential. However, recent changes demonstrate how difficult this process of trust-building can be.

This situation suggests that while technological cooperation between the U.S. and China remains possible, the process is becoming increasingly complex. The outcome of these negotiations is expected to have a significant impact on global AI technological development and related policies. Therefore, the progress of these negotiations will remain a crucial subject of observation for both technology companies and governments.

Ultimately, the US-China AI negotiations reflect the tension between technological competition and cooperation, and are expected to provide important direction for future global technology policy.
