While previous efforts were primarily focused on maximizing efficiency through technological innovation, this failure demands a fundamental reconsideration of how financial institutions should handle advanced technologies such as AI.

This change goes beyond a single company's strategic adjustment, suggesting an overall shift in the direction of financial technology policy. In particular, the recent shift from 'escalation' to 'de-escalation' reflects the movement of financial regulatory authorities and corporations toward a more cautious approach. This response following the AI investment failure can be interpreted not as a mere retreat, but as laying the foundation for more systematic and secure technological integration. 

This transition is expected to herald stricter verification procedures and strengthened regulations in future financial technology policies, accelerating the process of finding a balance between technological innovation and stability. Financial institutions must now establish strategies that include more thorough risk management and ethical considerations prior to implementing AI.
