The global energy market is entering a new phase of tension due to the lack of refining capacity. While this issue was expected to gradually ease over recent weeks, the situation is instead worsening. This can be interpreted as a significant turning point in the energy geopolitical landscape.

As recently as two days ago, international organizations assessed that progress was being made in efforts to stabilize the market, alongside response strategies for the refining capacity shortage. However, recent data and analysis are overturning these optimistic projections. A combination of declining refinery utilization rates, aging facilities, and a lack of new investment is increasing energy supply instability.

Several factors are working in combination behind this change. First, energy transition policies aimed at addressing climate change are limiting investment in existing refining facilities. Second, geopolitical uncertainty is increasing supply instability in major oil-producing regions such as the Middle East. As these factors converge, the lack of refining capacity is spreading beyond a simple economic issue into geopolitical pressure.

International organizations such as the IMF are warning of this situation, emphasizing the urgent need for international cooperation alongside improvements in energy efficiency and the development of alternative energy. Despite these warnings, current market trends remain unstable. If the shortage in refining capacity persists, there is an increasing possibility that energy prices will rise and competition between nations will intensify.

Accordingly, the escalating tension in the energy market is highly likely to act as a catalyst that triggers changes in the global geopolitical structure beyond a mere economic issue. Governments and international organizations must closely monitor these changes and seek proactive response strategies.
