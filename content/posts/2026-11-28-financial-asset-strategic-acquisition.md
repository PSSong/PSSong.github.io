Recently, there has been a prominent movement among major banks, including Goldman Sachs, to focus more on strategic asset acquisitions. This trend goes beyond the mere pursuit of economic profit; it reflects an intention to restructure the stability and network dynamics of financial markets during recessionary periods. While a conservative approach due to the recession previously prevailed, the recent shift in direction demonstrates a change toward a 'bullish' stance, which is interpreted as a signal that financial institutions are seeking opportunities even amidst crises.

This strategic concentration of assets holds significance beyond simply anticipating an increase in asset value. By expanding investments in specific assets, major banks reveal an intention to strengthen their influence and control within their networks. In particular, the movement to secure core elements of global financial infrastructure through cooperative relationships with international organizations foreshadows structural changes in the financial market. This pattern can be viewed as part of an effort to enhance the stability and efficiency of the financial system during periods of recession.

However, these movements may have a double-edged impact on market participants. On one hand, they provide opportunities for stable fund management and risk diversification; on the other hand, the possibility of causing market imbalances cannot be ruled out. Therefore, continuous observation is required regarding how major players in the financial market will manage and utilize these changes.
