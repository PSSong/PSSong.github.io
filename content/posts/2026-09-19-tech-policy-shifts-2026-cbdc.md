The possibility of introducing Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) is emerging as a critical factor that could reshape the future of the global financial system. In particular, the recent shift in this issue from 'escalation' to a neutral stance suggests that the tension between technological innovation and financial regulation is easing. This change demonstrates that policymakers are more carefully navigating the balance between technological progress and financial stability.

Discussions on the introduction of CBDCs raise important questions regarding the sustainability of the dollar system. The introduction of digital currencies can enhance the efficiency of international payment systems while simultaneously strengthening the execution of monetary policy and financial surveillance capabilities. However, these changes necessitate regulatory adjustments required to maintain trust and stability in the existing financial system.

The shift toward a neutral stance reflects the process of seeking harmony between technological progress and policy adjustment. While leveraging the potential benefits of digital currencies, policymakers are conducting thorough reviews to ensure financial system stability and privacy protection. This suggests that the introduction of CBDCs goes beyond a mere technical issue and could form a new paradigm for the international financial order and cross-border cooperation.

Ultimately, these changes will serve as an important indicator of how the global financial system will respond to future technological challenges. The shift toward a neutral stance reflects the process of exploring a sustainable path between technological advancement and regulatory adjustment, providing deep insight into the future direction of financial policy.
