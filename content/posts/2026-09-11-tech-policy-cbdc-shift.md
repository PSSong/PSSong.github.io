Recently, as the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) has strengthened its requirements for security updates regarding Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC), discussions on the possibility of their introduction are heating up once again. This issue, which was dominated by positive outlooks just 15 days ago, has recently transitioned into an 'escalation' phase, presenting a more complex landscape in the process of finding a balance between technological progress and financial stability.

The previous 'bullish' sentiment reflected the perception of CBDC as an innovative tool capable of enhancing the efficiency of the financial system and adapting to the new digital economic environment. However, recent changes have arisen due to growing concerns regarding security, privacy, and the maintenance of financial system stability. The BIS's requirements extend beyond mere technical aspects, emphasizing the need for cooperation models between central banks and technology companies, as well as international regulatory frameworks.

This shift in direction simultaneously demonstrates the challenges and opportunities faced by the financial system during its adaptation to digital innovation. While the introduction of CBDC may accelerate the proliferation of digital currencies, it also carries the potential to cause increased cybersecurity threats and heightened complexity within the financial system. Consequently, there is an increasing need for central banks and policymakers to focus on maintaining financial stability while keeping pace with technological advancements.

Experts observe that these changes may demand a new paradigm for the global financial order and international cooperation, moving beyond simple technical issues. Future discussions are expected to focus on the innovative benefits brought by CBDC introduction, alongside the establishment of international cooperation and regulatory frameworks to manage it safely.

In this context, the possibility of CBDC introduction is positioning itself as a significant turning point in the process of finding a balance between technological progress and financial stability. Future trends are expected to play a decisive role in determining the future of the financial system.
