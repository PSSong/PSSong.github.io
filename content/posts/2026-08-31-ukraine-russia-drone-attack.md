Over the past three days, as Ukrainian drone attacks have targeted Russia's energy infrastructure, Russia has been facing significant challenges in terms of energy security. These attacks can be interpreted as more than mere military clashes; they are signs of further escalating geopolitical tensions. In particular, attacks on energy facilities act as factors that directly impact Russia's economic and social stability.

The background to these attacks lies in the sustained state of tension following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. By targeting Russia's strategic resources, Ukraine is revealing its intention to weaken Russia's capability to conduct war. However, this strategy may increase the possibility of Russian retaliation, which in turn risks amplifying instability within energy supply chains.

The impact of these drone attacks extends beyond simple physical damage, contributing to the creation of fear and anxiety within Russia. This, coupled with the Russian government's efforts to maintain public support, provides momentum for seeking even more hardline responses. Therefore, these attacks serve to pressure Russia into taking measures to strengthen its energy security while simultaneously increasing uncertainty in international energy markets.

This situation is highly likely to influence the energy security strategies of East Asia and Europe amidst the competitive landscape between the U.S. and China. As instability in Russia's energy supply increases, countries in these regions are expected to focus more intensely on finding alternative energy sources. Consequently, Ukraine's drone attacks are emerging as a new variable in global energy geopolitics, transcending being merely a regional issue.
