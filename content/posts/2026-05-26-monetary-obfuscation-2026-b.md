Recently, trends of widening credit spreads and shrinking liquidity have become prominent in financial markets. These data points stand in stark contrast to the market's optimistic narratives, raising concerns among investors and economists. A widening credit spread suggests increasing credit risk for corporations and individuals, while liquidity contraction is interpreted as a warning signal that the flow of funds within the financial system is being constrained.

However, this phenomenon requires a more complex interpretation when coupled with recent shifts in central bank policy stances. Until the 16th, a 'hawkish'—or tightening—monetary policy was emphasized, but a subsequent shift toward a 'dovish'—or easing—policy stance has occurred. While this transition may appear to be an attempt to provide immediate stability to the market, a gap with actual data still persists.

The background of this shift is likely driven by global economic uncertainty and easing inflationary pressures. As central banks strive to maintain economic growth and the pressure to manage inflation eases, a move toward a more accommodative monetary policy stance seems to have taken place. However, it is noteworthy that these policy changes have not fully resolved the actual market credit stress and liquidity issues.

In particular, the widening of credit spreads suggests that corporations and financial institutions are experiencing difficulties in raising funds, which could negatively impact long-term economic growth. Liquidity contraction signifies a situation where funds are not flowing smoothly within the financial system, which can be interpreted as a precursor to a financial crisis.

Ultimately, there appear to be limits to how much central bank policy changes can support the market's optimistic narrative. Market participants need to carefully observe the discrepancy between policy changes and actual economic indicators. This mismatch implies structural credit crunches and liquidity risks, necessitating a more cautious approach to the future economic outlook.
