As the movement toward strengthening AI regulation accelerates globally, policy changes in United Airlines and major financial institutions are more clearly demonstrating the complex network connections of this shift. This suggests that beyond a mere matter of technological regulation, the power structures of the global economy and industry are being readjusted.

Until 17 days ago, the approach to AI regulation maintained a relatively optimistic and positive 'bullish' stance. However, the recent shift in direction is an 'escalation,' showing that tensions within the industry are rising alongside stricter regulations. The adjustment of United Airlines' AI utilization policies and the movement by major banks to strengthen data security are direct reflections of these changes.

The case of United Airlines shows that approaches to improving customer service and operational efficiency through AI technology are being adjusted in response to strengthening regulations. This suggests that the use of AI technology within the aviation industry is expanding beyond simple efficiency improvements into the realm of legal and ethical responsibility.

A similar pattern is observed in the financial sector as well. By introducing policies to enhance the transparency and security of AI-based systems, major banks are once again emphasizing how crucial the expectations and requirements of regulatory authorities are during the digital transformation of financial services. These changes imply that the balance of power within the financial industry is being readjusted alongside the strengthening of AI regulation.

These changes in power structures go beyond mere technological regulation, reflecting the process by which global corporations form new cooperative and competitive relationships as they adapt to the regulatory environment. Moving forward, continuous observation will be necessary to see how these changes in network connections will impact the global AI ecosystem.

In conclusion, the strengthening of AI regulation goes beyond simple technical adjustments and heralds a reorganization of complex relationships and power structures among companies. These changes will serve as important clues in understanding future policy decisions and industrial trends.
