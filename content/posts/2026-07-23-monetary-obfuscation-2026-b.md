As global economic uncertainty intensifies, a notable divergence between the Dollar Index and high-yield (HY) credit spreads has recently emerged. Typically, a rising Dollar Index reflects increased demand for safe-haven assets, which is interpreted as an indicator of economic instability. Conversely, widening credit spreads suggest an increase in corporate credit risk. However, these two indicators have recently been pointing in different directions, demonstrating a mismatch between the economic narrative and actual data.

This phenomenon, which has persisted for the past 30 days, is closely related to the shift in central banks' monetary policy directions. Initially, a 'hawkish' stance predominated, but a transition toward a neutral position has recently been observed. This can be interpreted as part of central banks' efforts to mitigate excessive market anxiety, despite the continued high level of uncertainty regarding economic conditions. Nevertheless, despite this transition, the divergence between the Dollar Index and credit spreads is sending confusing signals regarding the economic outlook.

Such divergence provides important implications for investors and policymakers. It suggests that an approach beyond simple indicator interpretation is necessary to understand the complexity of the economy and the interactions between various factors. Therefore, to predict future economic trends, it will be necessary to closely analyze the intercorrelations and changes among diverse indicators.
