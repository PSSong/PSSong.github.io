In recent weeks, the international community has been accelerating policy changes to address climate change. These changes signal the arrival of a new climate regime that goes beyond simple environmental protection to form a new paradigm for economic structures and global cooperation. Governments and international organizations are further specifying carbon neutrality goals and actively introducing policies to promote the transition to renewable energy.

In particular, the European Union has set a goal to reduce carbon emissions by 55% compared to 1990 levels by 2030 and is introducing specific legislation to achieve this. The United States is also intensifying its response to climate change through increased investment in clean energy and strengthened carbon emission regulations following its reentry into the Paris Agreement. These movements are being interpreted as more than just environmental policies; they are seen as economic opportunities. The growth of the green economy is highly likely to lead to the creation of new industrial sectors and jobs.

However, these changes may cause conflicts between existing industrial structures and stakeholders. The structural shift in the fossil fuel industry carries the risk of job losses and economic uncertainty. Consequently, there are growing calls for the strengthening of social safety nets and the implementation of retraining programs to accompany the policy transition.

The establishment of a new climate regime further highlights the importance of international cooperation. It is essential that developing countries receive the necessary technological and financial support to respond to climate change. This will lay the foundation for all nations to collectively overcome the climate crisis and move toward a sustainable future.

Ultimately, these policy changes signify more than just solving environmental issues; they represent a fundamental restructuring of the global economy and social structure. The policy choices of individual nations and the direction of international cooperation are expected to play a decisive role in future climate change responses and the establishment of a new climate regime.
