June 14, 2026—The global financial market is experiencing heightened de-risking sentiment due to the widening of credit spreads and the rise in the Volatility Index (VIX). While these indicators reflect investors' tendency to avoid risk, the Fed's recent shift in policy direction and the complexity of the international financial structure continue to add uncertainty to the market.

The Fed, which had maintained a 'hawkish' stance until recently, has transitioned toward a neutral direction. This suggests the possibility of adjustments that consider economic flexibility, moving away from past aggressive tightening policies. However, this transition is acting as a factor that could cause market confusion. The widening of credit spreads indicates an increase in risk perception within the corporate bond market, reflecting concerns about economic growth. On the other hand, the rise in the VIX index shows that investors are becoming more vigilant regarding future uncertainty.

Nevertheless, sector rotation in certain areas and positive outlooks from some financial institutions continue to maintain risk-on factors. These institutions are seeking opportunities even amidst economic uncertainty, which partially reflects the risk appetite of market participants. In particular, the trend of the DXY (Dollar Index) is becoming a crucial indicator for understanding the role of the US dollar in international financial flows.

The possibility of Fed policy adjustments and changes in the international financial structure are expected to play a decisive role in future market trends. Investors must carefully observe the Fed's next moves and the resulting changes in global capital flows. In this complex situation, the market continues to balance between uncertainty and opportunity.
