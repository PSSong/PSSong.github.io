Over the last three days, financial markets have experienced a shift from 'dovish' to 'hawkish.' This transition is more than a simple change in policy stance; it is serving as a catalyst to re-examine the perception of structural threats to the market. The continuous contraction of net liquidity may superficially appear to increase risk appetite, but in reality, it suggests that pressure is mounting within the market's underlying structure.

Previously, the central bank's dovish policy stance provided market stability, but the recent reversal highlights structural pressures, such as the widening of credit spreads. Changes in the credit market imply rising financing costs for corporations and financial institutions, which may negatively impact long-term economic growth.

This shift in direction prompts a re-evaluation of how central bank policy decisions influence the market's liquidity environment. The acceleration of liquidity contraction is likely to not only heighten short-term financial market volatility but also serve as a factor that could constrain economic stability and growth potential in the long run.

Consequently, investors and economic experts must closely monitor not only the immediate effects of liquidity contraction but also the subsequent changes in credit markets and structural pressures. In this light, the reversal of liquidity contraction can be interpreted as a warning regarding the fundamental stability of the market, transcending a mere shift in policy stance.
