May 4, 2026—the European Union's (EU) climate policy is showing a shift from eco-friendly policies to a pragmatic approach amidst recent energy crises and economic pressures. This transition can be interpreted as a complex strategy that goes beyond simply pursuing environmental goals, simultaneously considering economic stability and energy security.

Over the past few weeks, the EU has faced energy supply instability due to the conflict between Russia and Ukraine. As gas and oil prices surged, the perception has spread that existing green energy transition policies are acting as an economic burden. In this situation, some member states are seeking ways to temporarily increase reliance on fossil fuels in the short term while maintaining investments in renewable energy.

For example, Germany and France are considering the reactivation of coal-fired power plants for short-term energy security, which appears to conflict with some principles of eco-friendly policy. While these measures are interpreted as a pragmatic approach to overcoming the short-term energy crisis, they face difficulties in balancing with long-term climate goals.

Nevertheless, the EU still adheres to its 2050 carbon neutrality goal. This change appears to be a process of finding a complex balance between short-term economic pressures and long-term environmental goals. Policymakers are continuing efforts to bridge the gap between the pragmatic approach and the green transition through improving energy efficiency, developing alternative energy technologies, and strengthening international cooperation.

Ultimately, these policy changes in Europe reflect the complexity of global climate policy and are expected to serve as an important testing ground for finding a sustainable balance between economic stability and environmental protection.
