As trade disputes between China and South Korea continue, this issue has taken on a more complex context beyond simple economic issues, situated within the geopolitical competition structure between the US and China. The escalating tension between the two countries is prompting a reassessment of the potential impact on the South Korean economy, particularly leading to a reconsideration of South Korea's position in the context of the Indo-Pacific strategy.

As China is a major trading partner for South Korea, the economic ties between the two nations are very deep. However, as China has recently strengthened sanctions and trade restrictions on South Korean companies, South Korean firms are moving toward restructuring supply chains and exploring alternative markets. While these changes may lead to increased costs and decreased productivity in the short term, they could also serve as an opportunity for South Korea to develop a more diversified economic structure in the long term.

In particular, within the framework of US-China competition, South Korea continues its efforts to maintain a neutral stance, but balancing China's pressure with its role as an ally of the United States is becoming increasingly difficult. This deepens the deliberation over what role South Korea will play within the Indo-Pacific strategy. The South Korean government and businesses are considering various scenarios to respond to these geopolitical shifts, which is expected to have a significant impact on future trade policies and the direction of international cooperation.

Ultimately, trade tensions between China and South Korea are becoming a critical testing ground for how South Korea will redefine its economic position within the global geopolitical environment, moving beyond mere economic issues. In this context, South Korea appears to need to focus on finding a balance between economic stability and geopolitical flexibility.
