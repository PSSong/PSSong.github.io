Over the past three months, Europe has been experiencing a severe energy crisis due to rising energy prices and supply instability. Amidst this crisis, the European Union is actively pursuing policies to accelerate the transition to renewable energy. This is part of an effort to simultaneously strengthen environmental protection and energy security. However, this transition process goes beyond mere technical and economic aspects, causing complex changes in international relations.

In the short term, the transition to renewable energy is adding tension to relationships with countries that relied on fossil fuels. Specifically, traditional energy suppliers such as Russia and Middle Eastern nations may feel economic pressure from Europe's changes. This could potentially increase geopolitical uncertainty related to energy security.

On the other hand, in the long term, it provides an opportunity for Europe to secure global leadership in the green technology sector. However, this comes with significant economic burdens alongside massive investments. Building renewable energy infrastructure and transitioning existing energy systems requires vast amounts of capital, which can strain national budgets and corporate finances.

Europe's movements are not merely about overcoming the energy crisis but are also playing a role in promoting structural changes in the global energy market. Nevertheless, the economic burdens and shifts in international relations arising from the renewable energy transition pose important questions regarding how Europe will strike a balance moving forward. While this transition is essential for Europe to secure a sustainable position in the future energy regime, the challenges involved are equally significant.
