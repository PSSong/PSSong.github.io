The recent trend of tightening restrictions on Artificial Intelligence (AI) access within the financial industry carries deeper implications beyond mere security enhancement. A key factor behind financial institutions strengthening regulations on AI usage is the need to ensure technological transparency and accountability. However, these changes also raise the possibility that this is part of a preparatory process for the introduction of digital currencies, specifically Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC).

In particular, the recent increase in the price volatility of gold and silver suggests that discussions regarding the introduction of CBDC are becoming more active within the financial markets. Gold and silver have traditionally been recognized as stores of value; as their price fluctuations expand, observations suggest that central banks are considering a transition toward stable digital assets. In this context, CBDC is gaining attention as a stable store of value that could potentially replace existing physical currencies and gold.

Furthermore, while AI access restrictions are being tightened, FinTech companies are focusing on developing more transparent and regulatory-compliant AI systems. This shift can be interpreted as a process of finding a balance between technological innovation and regulation. It is also noteworthy that as central banks review the introduction of CBDC, they are focusing on establishing the technical and legal foundations for compatibility with existing financial systems and user protection.

In conclusion, the strengthening of AI regulations by financial institutions and the fluctuations in gold/silver prices are likely not merely isolated issues, but part of a larger picture for the digital transformation of the global financial system. This provides important implications regarding how changes in technological regulation and digital currency policy are interconnected and how they may reshape the future financial landscape.
